# Funding and compute

Status verified **28 July 2026**. Deadlines move; re-check before applying.

A warning before the table. Money is not this project's binding constraint —
**reviewer attention is**. A grant that buys API credits without buying
mathematicians produces more unreviewed nodes, which makes the graph worse, not
better. See [RISKS.md](RISKS.md#the-grant-treadmill). Apply for funding when
there is something a reviewer would want to read, not in order to create it.

## Competitions — visibility, not funding

- **[AIMO Progress Prizes](https://aimoprize.com/)** — Progress Prize 3 closed Apr 2026 (winners Jun 2026); the **Proof Pilot** concluded Jul 2026. No PP4 announced, but the cadence makes a late-2026 launch plausible. Not a grant. Indirect value: a clean open proof-structure dataset is exactly what AIMO teams want for retrieval.
- **[SAIR Foundation](https://competition.sair.foundation/)** — the "SAIR" in the notes. Terence Tao on the board. Its **Mathematics Distillation Challenge** asks entrants to compress 22M universal-algebra results from Tao's Equational Theories Project into a ≤10KB prompt; Stage 2 requires formal proofs or counterexamples. No cash, but arguably the **highest strategic alignment** on this page — the competitions are literally about structuring formalized theorem corpora for AI, plus direct contact with Tao's formal-maths network.

## Applying honestly

Whatever is written in an application will be read by someone who knows this
field. Three things follow.

**Do not claim novelty that [PRIOR-ART.md](PRIOR-ART.md) contradicts.** A
reviewer for the AI for Math Fund knows what Metamath, the Stacks Project and
LeanDojo are. Claiming to be the first to connect axioms to theorems ends the
review. Say instead what is actually unoccupied, and cite the things that are
not.

**Do not put Millennium Problems in the application.** See
[RISKS.md](RISKS.md#overclaiming). The measurable goal should be something a
reviewer can check in twelve months.

**Budget review, not just tokens.** The most credible line item this project can
put in a budget is paying graduate students to *check* nodes. That is the scarce
input, it is unglamorous, and saying so signals that the applicant understands
their own problem.
